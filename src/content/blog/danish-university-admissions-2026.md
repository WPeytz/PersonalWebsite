Today, applicants across Denmark received their university admission
decisions. The national results contain a huge amount of information, but it is
spread across tables and reports that make the bigger picture difficult to see.

I built [**Danish University Admissions 2026**](https://www.williampeytz.com/UniversityAdmissions2026)
to make that picture easier to explore.

## The national picture

This year's coordinated admissions system recorded:

- **58,673 admitted students**
- **84,036 first-choice applications**
- **957 programmes**
- **3,925 standby offers**

Those headline figures are only the beginning. Behind them are large
differences in demand, capacity, and admission requirements across
institutions, subjects, and parts of the country.

## An interactive admissions atlas

The atlas brings the 2026 results together in one bilingual, interactive view.
You can compare institutions, explore programme demand and admission cutoffs,
see geographic patterns on the map, and find programmes that still have open
study places.

I wanted it to answer questions that are awkward to investigate in a
spreadsheet:

- Where is demand highest relative to available places?
- Which institutions and subjects attract the most first-choice applicants?
- How do admission cutoffs vary across programmes?
- Where is there still unused capacity?

The result is less of a dashboard and more of an atlas: a way to move between
the national overview and individual programmes without losing the wider
context.

## Where ambition meets capacity

University admissions are ultimately an allocation problem with very human
consequences. Every cutoff represents both student ambition and a finite number
of places. Looking at demand and capacity together makes it easier to understand
why some programmes are highly competitive while others are still looking for
students.
