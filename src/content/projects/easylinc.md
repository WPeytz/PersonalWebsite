## Overview

EasyLINC 2.0 is a ride-ordering solution designed specifically for elderly
users. The brief: take a category of app that is normally optimized for
young, sighted, dexterous users and re-design it for people who aren't.

## What I did

- **Research first.** Interviewed older users, mapped pain points with
  existing transport apps (small targets, jargon, too many steps,
  unclear feedback).
- **Iterative prototyping.** Sketches → wireframes → mid-fidelity Figma
  prototypes → testable flows. Each cycle was reviewed against the
  accessibility constraints we'd surfaced in research.
- **User testing.** Walked target users through real tasks on the
  prototype and watched where they hesitated, missed buttons, or
  misinterpreted state.

## What I learned

The biggest accessibility wins came from things that look "boring" on
paper: bigger touch targets, plain language, shorter flows, generous
confirmation. The fancy interaction design barely mattered compared to
removing assumptions the original product had baked in.
